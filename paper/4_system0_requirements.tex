This section states the postulates that select the substrate and derives their consequences. Each postulate is a formal reading of a PM requirement for a closed system. None names $E_8$, a rank, or a target number.

\subsection{Addresses and Cost}
\label{sec:finiteness}
\textbf{Postulate L.} Microscopic addresses form a full-rank lattice $\Lambda\cong\mathbb Z^n$ whose translations preserve the local update, with an integral symmetric configuration cost $B(x,y)=x^{\mathsf T}Ay$ and $Q(x)=B(x,x)$. The cost is integral because, by Closure, it counts transitions.

\textbf{Postulate F (Finiteness).} For every $c$, the set $\{x\in\Lambda: Q(x)\le c\}$ is finite.

For an integral symmetric form, F holds if and only if $A$ is positive definite (Lean: \texttt{posDef\_iff\_finite\_levels}). An indefinite cost would allow infinitely many configurations at bounded cost.

\subsection{Readability and Self-Duality}
\label{sec:readability}
By Closure a probe is a substrate state $y$, and reading a state $x$ with it yields the pairing $B(x,y)$. The smallest distinction is one bit.

\textbf{Postulate R (Conservation under Closure).} Every one-bit distinction of the substrate is readable by the substrate at one-bit resolution: if $B(x,y)$ is even for every probe $y\in\Lambda$, then $x\in2\Lambda$.

\begin{theorem}
Postulate R holds if and only if $\det A$ is odd.
\end{theorem}
The states invisible to every probe at one-bit resolution form $(\Lambda\cap2\Lambda^*)/2\Lambda$, the $2$-torsion of the discriminant group $\Lambda^*/\Lambda$ (Lean: \texttt{bitReadable\_iff\_odd}). An undetectable label would be a distinction the closed substrate carries but cannot maintain, since nothing inside it can read it.

\textbf{Postulate A (Map).} Addresses are read as bits without an external translation table: every glue element is its own inverse, $2\Lambda^*\subseteq\Lambda$.

\begin{theorem}
Postulates R and A together imply $\det A=\pm1$: the substrate is unimodular, $\Lambda=\Lambda^*$.
\end{theorem}
By A, $\Lambda^*/\Lambda$ is $2$-torsion; by R it has no $2$-torsion; so it is trivial (Lean: \texttt{bitAddressed\_bitReadable\_unimodular}). Equivalently, $\det A\cdot\det(2A^{-1})=2^n$ with $\det A$ odd.

Self-duality is thus not stated directly. It follows from two requirements about how a closed system reads its own addresses. Each alone is weaker: $D_4$ satisfies A but not R, and $A_2$ satisfies R but not A (\Cref{tab:independence}).

\subsection{Exchange and Evenness}
\label{sec:even}
Translation operations $U_x$ act on the substrate and compose up to a central phase. Their exchange law $U_xU_y=\varepsilon^{C(x,y)}U_yU_x$, with $\varepsilon$ of order two, defines a bi-additive form $C$ with values in $\mathbb Z/2$. An operation always commutes with itself, so $C(x,x)=0$.

\textbf{Postulate X (Protocol and Causality).} Some pair of operations fails to commute ($C\neq0$): the order of operations is observable somewhere. Operations whose addresses have zero pairing commute: coordination requires a coupling channel.

\begin{theorem}
Postulate X implies that every norm $Q(x)$ is even.
\end{theorem}
If $Q(v)$ is odd, then $w=Q(v)z-B(v,z)v$ is orthogonal to $v$ for every $z$, so locality gives $Q(v)C(v,z)=C(v,w)=0$ and hence $C(v,\cdot)=0$. Additivity of norm parity spreads this to every row, so $C=0$, contradicting nontriviality (Lean: \texttt{orthogonal\_nontrivial\_exchange\_forces\_even}). Locality is what excludes the parity-corrected exchange law $B(x,y)-Q(x)Q(y)\bmod2$, which is consistent on odd lattices but makes orthogonal operations anticommute.

\subsection{Economy and the Selection of \texorpdfstring{$E_8$}{E8}}
\label{sec:lattice_selection}
Postulates F, R, A, X say exactly that $A$ is positive definite, even, unimodular, and nonzero:

\begin{theorem}[Equivalence]
A substrate satisfies F, R, A, and X if and only if its lattice is positive definite, even, unimodular, and of positive rank.
\end{theorem}
(Lean: \texttt{Vacuum.geometric\_iff}.) The converse direction supplies the witnesses: $2\det A\cdot\operatorname{adj}A$ realizes A, and the reduced pairing realizes X.

\textbf{Postulate M (Economy).} No substrate satisfying F, R, A, and X has smaller rank. This is PM's selection principle: cost grows with the number of independently maintained coordinates, and surviving histories concentrate on least cost.

\begin{theorem}
A nonzero positive-definite even unimodular lattice has rank divisible by eight.
\end{theorem}
The proof is the theta-series argument\cite{serre_course_1973,conway_sphere_1988}. Evenness gives $\Theta(s-i)=\Theta(s)$; unimodularity and Poisson summation give $\Theta(1/s)=s^{n/2}\Theta(s)$; following $1\to1+i\to(1+i)/2\to1$ yields $\Theta(1)=x^n\Theta(1)$ with $x^2=-i$, and $\Theta(1)>0$ forces $8\mid n$ (Lean: \texttt{rank\_dvd\_eight}, with $n$-dimensional Poisson summation proved by induction on the dimension).

\begin{theorem}
Up to integral isometry, the only positive-definite even unimodular lattice of rank eight is $E_8$.
\end{theorem}
The theta series is a modular form of weight four for $SL_2(\mathbb Z)$, hence equal to the Eisenstein series $E_4$; this gives $240$ roots and $2160$ vectors of norm four. A frame of eight orthogonal norm-four vectors congruent modulo $2\Lambda$ spans a $D_8$, and evenness with unimodularity fixes the glue to $E_8$ (Lean: \texttt{thetaMF\_eq\_E}$_4$, \texttt{card\_norm\_four}, \texttt{e8\_unique}).

Since $E_8$ satisfies F, R, A, and X, Economy bounds the rank by eight; divisibility forces rank eight; uniqueness gives $E_8$ (Lean: \texttt{Vacuum.geometric\_selects\_e8}). Conversely $E_8$ is economical, so the selection is consistent and unique (\texttt{substrate\_exists\_unique}). Its packing optimality\cite{viazovska_sphere_2017} is not used.
