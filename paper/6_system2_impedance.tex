We now price the persistence of a minimal defect on the forced substrate. Each of the six pillars contributes one term; each term is computed from the integers of System 1.

\subsection{Costs as Expected Transitions}
\label{subsec:additive_theorem}
PM prices a pillar as toll times frequency, $Z_i=c_0f_i$, sums the pillars, and only then exponentiates: a history of action $S$ survives with probability $e^{-\beta S}$\cite{kate_meyer_persistence_2026}. Three consequences fix how the terms combine.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item \textbf{Each term is an expected count.} A pillar's cost per cycle is the expected number of transitions it accounts for. For indicator events, expected counts add, each weighted by the single toll $c_0$ (Lean: \texttt{expected\_count\_linear}); Closure sets $c_0=1$. A probability therefore enters as itself, not as $-\log p$, and the terms add with unit weight. The exponential applies to the total: survival is the product of per-pillar factors $e^{-\beta Z_i}$ (\texttt{survival\_factorizes}).
\item \textbf{Signs follow the pillar definitions.} Capacity, Margin, and Map provision, and Toll spends; their transitions add. Governor restricts which transitions occur and Protocol coordinates them; both act on other pillars' transitions and create none. Restriction keeps a subset and coordination merges equivalent attempts, so neither can increase the count, and a removed transition lowers it by exactly its probability (\texttt{restriction\_lowers}, \texttt{merging\_lowers}, \texttt{removal\_subtracts}). Each of Conservation and Causality has one regulating pillar; Finiteness has none (\texttt{NetIndex.signs}).
\item \textbf{Uniform measures.} Where a term is a probability over a finite label set that the substrate cannot distinguish internally, Closure forces the uniform measure (\Cref{sec:the_test_case}).
\end{enumerate}
The index is therefore the net number of transitions per cycle, provisioned minus removed (\texttt{index\_as\_net\_transitions}):
\begin{equation}
Z_{\rm geo}=Z_{\rm CAP}+Z_{\rm MAP}+Z_{\rm TOL}+Z_{\rm MAR}-|Z_{\rm PRO}|-|Z_{\rm GOV}|.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Capacity}
\label{subsec:alpha_cap}
A defect's identity is its set of distinctions from the vacuum. Let the maintenance cost be local, measured from the vacuum, and unchanged by exchanging the names of the two states (by Closure, which state is called ``vacuum'' is not intrinsic). Then the cost is one density times the number of neighbouring pairs in different states (Lean: \texttt{only\_distinctions\_cost}). Uniform regions, the defect's interior and the surrounding vacuum, cost nothing; only the boundary costs. An area cost satisfies the first two conditions but not the third (\texttt{area\_breaks\_relabeling}). At speed one, one unit of boundary is one tick, so the density is one transition.

The defect's envelope lies in the causal plane. A signal must cross it and return within one cycle, so its width in every direction is at most $\Delta$. For a convex envelope with support function $h$, the width in direction $\theta$ is $h(\theta)+h(\theta+\pi)$ and the perimeter is $\int_0^{2\pi}h$. Width at most $w$ in every direction gives perimeter at most $\pi w$, with equality for every envelope of constant width $w$, whatever its shape (Barbier's theorem; Lean: \texttt{perimeter\_le}, \texttt{barbier}). Capacity is the allocation the causal bound permits:
\begin{equation}
Z_{\rm CAP}=\pi\Delta\approx\num{\AlphaInvCAPVal}
\end{equation}
(\texttt{capacity\_is\_max}). Neither roundness nor saturation is a separate assumption.

\subsection{Map}
\label{subsec:alpha_map}
Map retains the boundary that defines the defect. For any closed triangulated surface, the total angle defect is $2\pi(V-E+F)=2\pi\chi$, independent of triangulation, shape, and size (Descartes' theorem, the discrete Gauss--Bonnet theorem; Lean: \texttt{descartes}). So $\chi$ is the number of full $2\pi$ turns the boundary makes to close: two for the sphere (\texttt{sphere\_two\_turns}). Counting one transition per closure,
\begin{equation}
Z_{\rm MAP}=\chi=\AlphaInvMAPVal.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Protocol}
\label{subsec:alpha_pro}
The defect's embedding occupies $\sigma$ of the $R_M$ addresses of a cycle; any injective placement leaves exactly $R_M-\sigma=167$ residual addresses (Lean: \texttt{residual\_card}). The update is a function, so a state has exactly one successor. Protocol coordinates that successor among the residual addresses without an external pointer; the coordination event has the uniform probability of a single address, $2^{-I}$ with $I=\log_2167$ the information needed to name it (\texttt{protocol\_persistence}). It removes one transition when it occurs:
\begin{equation}
Z_{\rm PRO}=-\frac{1}{R_M-\sigma}=-\frac{1}{167}\approx\num{\AlphaInvPROVal}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Governor}
\label{subsec:alpha_gov}
Governor restricts transitions using the boundary's turning budget of $\chi$ closures per cycle. No slot of the cycle is preferred, so a credit distributed over the $\Delta$ slots that is invariant under advancing the clock is uniform, with $\chi/\Delta$ per slot (Lean: \texttt{governor\_uniform}). It removes transitions:
\begin{equation}
Z_{\rm GOV}=-\frac{\chi}{\Delta}=-\frac{2}{43}\approx\num{\AlphaInvGOVVal}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Toll}
\label{subsec:alpha_tol}
Toll is the probability that a transition preserves the defect.

\paragraph{Three legs.} A leg's state is what the substrate observes locally about its channel: active or idle, one bit (Postulate I; Lean: \texttt{leg\_state\_binary}, \texttt{leg\_factorization}). By Closure the legs carry no external labels, so an interaction treats them symmetrically; by Conservation it is reversible. Every such two-leg update relabels each leg by the same map, possibly exchanging them, so no leg depends on the other (\texttt{two\_legs\_separable}). A symmetric reversible three-leg update exists that couples the legs (\texttt{three\_legs\_interact}). Economy takes the least arity that coordinates: three. (With three-valued legs two would suffice, \texttt{three\_valued\_two\_legs\_interact}, so the one-bit leg is essential.)

\paragraph{Valid transitions.} The internal configuration of a vertex is its three legs, each one of $N$ channels, and its interaction direction, one of $\sigma$. A transition preserves the defect when the configuration returns to the reference up to the defect's one invisible symmetry, reversal of boundary orientation. A connected orientable boundary has exactly two orientations (\texttt{two\_orientations}), related only by the flip (\texttt{orientation\_relabelings}), so exactly two configurations are valid (\texttt{toll\_identity}). The target address must also avoid the defect's own embedding (Causality: no self-aliasing), which a uniform target does with probability $(R_M-\sigma)/R_M$ (\texttt{nonaliasing\_prob}). Under the uniform measure,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
Z_{\rm TOL}&=\frac{2}{N^3\sigma}\cdot\frac{R_M-\sigma}{R_M}=\frac{334}{32^3\cdot5\cdot172}\\
&\approx\num{\AlphaInvTOLVal}
\end{split}
\end{equation}
(\texttt{toll\_persistence}). The factor $2$ is the number of orientations; it equals $\chi$ only because the boundary is a sphere.

\subsection{Margin}
\label{subsec:alpha_mar}
Margin is the resolution floor: one configuration among all the configurations of a localized defect. These are the product of three independent domains.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Description:} $L_{\rm embed}=31$ coordinates (\Cref{par:lembed}).
\item \textbf{Local interaction:} a connected subgraph on $\sigma$ edges localized to one neighbourhood is a tree, and any tree with $\sigma$ edges has $\sigma+1=6$ vertices; radius one makes it the star (Lean: \texttt{tree\_vertices}, \texttt{radius\_one\_star}).
\item \textbf{Causal cone:} at speed one in the causal plane, tick $t$ of a cycle reaches $2t+1$ positions, so one cycle's causal cone holds $\sum_{t<\Delta}(2t+1)=\Delta^2$ events (\texttt{causal\_cone\_card}).
\end{itemize}
Under the uniform measure,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
Z_{\rm MAR}&=\frac{1}{L_{\rm embed}(\sigma+1)\Delta^2}=\frac{1}{31\cdot6\cdot43^2}\\
&\approx\num{\AlphaInvMARVal}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}

\begin{split}
Z_{\rm geo}={}&\pi\Delta+\chi-\frac1{R_M-\sigma}-\frac\chi\Delta\\

\begin{theorem}[Main theorem]
Under the postulates of \Cref{tab:postulates}, the substrate is $E_8$ with two $D_4$ sectors, $\Delta=43$, all integer inputs take the values of System 1, and
\begin{align*}
137.035999212381397211&<Z_{\rm geo},\\
Z_{\rm geo}&<137.035999212381397213.
\end{align*}
The postulates are jointly satisfiable.
\end{theorem}
(Lean: \texttt{Main.persistence\_selects}, \texttt{Main.postulates\_satisfiable}.) The bounds use exact rational arithmetic and mathlib's proved bounds on $\pi$. No continuous parameter enters.

\paragraph{Premises.} The theorem rests on the postulates and on PM's definitions of the pillars. Where a step above reads a PM definition, that reading is stated: Governor restricts transitions and Protocol coordinates them; a vertex sees only local observations (Postulate I); a defect's description consists of the fields listed in \Cref{par:lembed}; its only invisible symmetry is orientation reversal. Everything else is proved.

\paragraph{Causal coupling.} Define $\kappa=1/Z_{\rm geo}$, the substrate's net admittance per cycle. Lean proves $0<\kappa<1$ (\texttt{causalCoupling\_bounds}).
